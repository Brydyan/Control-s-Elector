Privacy Policy

Version {{VERSION}} — effective from 6 November 2026.

This document explains what personal data the Control's Elector system processes, on what legal basis, for how long it keeps it, and what rights registered people have. It is the information required by Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR.

1. Data controller

Identity: Control's Elector — observation and results-recording platform for the Province of Santa Elena, Ecuador.

Scope: the platform operates on control.brydyan.me and its API on api.brydyan.me.

Data protection contact: through the Data Protection Officer (DPO) contact form described in section 6. No DPO email address is published in this document because enquiries are centralised in that form, which keeps an auditable record of every request.

2. Data we collect

2.1 Account data

  • Email address.
  • First and last name.
  • Role in the electoral process (observer, coordinator, administrator, master).
  • Password: never stored in plain text, only as a bcrypt hash.
  • Registration date, account activation date, and date of the last invitation sent.
  • Accepted version of the terms of use and privacy policy, and when it was accepted.

2.2 Electoral process data

  • Recorded tally sheets: voting table (JRV), office, status, voters, blank votes, void votes, and registration date.
  • Vote breakdown per candidate on each tally sheet.
  • Photographic evidence of tally sheets, compressed (.webp).
  • Incident reports about polling stations: description, type, and attached evidence.
  • Audit log: who performed each operation and from which IP address.

2.3 Technical data

  • Connection IP address.
  • Browser user agent.
  • Session identifier and access logs.

The platform does not request special categories of data. The incident report form is free text and a user may volunteer sensitive information: that is a warning to users, not a processing of sensitive data. The system does not profile users and does not use their data for advertising.

3. Legal basis

  • Art. 6(1)(b) — Performance of a contract: processing the account and the tally sheets is necessary for a person to use the platform as an electoral observer.
  • Art. 6(1)(c) — Compliance with a legal obligation: retaining tally sheets and audit trails is required by applicable electoral law.
  • Art. 6(1)(a) — Consent: acceptance of the terms of use and this policy is recorded with version, timestamp, IP address, and user agent.

4. Who can access the data

  • The observer: only their own tally sheets, incidents, and account data.
  • The coordinator: tally sheets and incidents for the stations they supervise, in order to resolve and correct disputes.
  • The administrator: read access to tally sheets and incidents for monitoring; no ability to modify them.
  • The master: full operational access, including catalogue and user management.
  • The DPO: access to data protection requests and to the acceptance history, only as far as needed to handle them.

Access control is applied in two layers: authorisation on the server (every endpoint declares the roles that enable it) and row-level isolation (RLS) in the database. A role without permission receives 403, and the database still withholds rows that do not belong to it.

5. Retention

  • Tally sheets and evidence: kept for the period required by electoral law (around 7 years). They are not automatically deleted when an account is cancelled, because they form part of the electoral record.
  • Terms acceptance audit: kept for as long as the record it substantiates, so that what each person accepted, and when, can be demonstrated.
  • Sessions and access logs: up to 90 days.
  • Account and profile: while the account is active. Deletion requests are handled by the DPO, except for electoral data retained under a legal obligation.
  • Data exports: the record of each export is kept, so that the exercise of the right of access can be demonstrated.

6. Rights of data subjects (Arts. 15 to 22)

  • Art. 15 — Access: every registered person can obtain a complete copy of their data as JSON through the export endpoint of their own session — download my data — or request it from the DPO. The endpoint requires an active session (it returns 401 without a token) and allows one export every 24 hours. The system keeps an audit record of every export.
  • Art. 16 — Rectification: name, surname, and email corrections are requested from the DPO, who handles them with the system administration team.
  • Art. 17 — Erasure: account deletion can be requested through the DPO form. Data retained under a legal obligation (tally sheets, evidence, and audit trails) is kept as described in section 5.
  • Art. 18 — Restriction: processing can be restricted on request.
  • Art. 20 — Portability: the export format is machine-readable JSON, which allows it to be ported to another controller.
  • Art. 21 — Objection: people may object to processing by stating their reason; the DPO assesses it and replies.
  • Art. 22 — Complaints: to the competent supervisory authority.

DPO contact form: to exercise a right. The form accepts at most one request every 30 seconds per IP address.

7. Security

  • bcrypt password hashing; the session token lives only in memory.
  • The refresh token travels in an httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict cookie: application code cannot read it.
  • Encryption in transit (HTTPS) and at rest in the database and object storage.
  • Evidence is compressed and stored outside the database.
  • Audit logging of operations on tally sheets.

8. Changes to this policy

This policy is versioned. When a new version is published, the current version changes, the system stops treating the previous one as accepted, and every person must accept again before continuing to use the platform. The date and version number appear at the top of this document.
